The game runs on the Mac too: AudioDefence.app, in its own zip on the same release, updating itself like the Windows build. It speaks through VoiceOver, or through the system voice when VoiceOver is off, and Settings -> Speech has the system voice's own voice, rate, pitch and volume. The README's "On the Mac" says how to open it the first time and how to let VoiceOver speak for it.
SAPI 5 stops the moment you interrupt it, and no longer holds up the game while it speaks. Settings, Speech has a Modern audio output row, on by default; turn it off to let Windows play it as before.
Press Control to stop whatever is being read out, in the menus or in a game.
On the Endless screen you can no longer leave while the cards are being dealt. Escape, or Circle on a controller, keeps you there until they are down.
Guns are heard the moment you press, and the moment you let go. The Machine Gun's shot had an eighth of a second of silence recorded before the bang, and so did the sound of it winding down, which was the gap you heard when tapping it and when letting go after a burst. Every sound a weapon makes now starts where the sound starts; the recordings are untouched.
The Minigun power-up is heard for as long as it fires. Fully upgraded it runs twelve and a half seconds, where its recording is ten, so the gun went quiet for the last two and a half while the bullets kept landing. And a power-up you are holding stops when you die, instead of firing on into the death screen.
A gun that is still firing stops when you switch weapon or when you die. Switching away from the Micro SMG or the Machine Gun mid-burst used to leave the old gun firing away with nothing to stop it, and no reload was called out while that was happening.
When you hold the trigger until the clip is out, the gun stops before the Reload call-out rather than under it, and the call-out is always said at that moment - it used to be held back if anything had been called out in the last five seconds, which in a fight is most of the time. And a gun with no empty click of its own - the Machine Gun is one - now answers with its own warning instead of saying nothing: once for a press, and over and over while you hold the trigger down. The low ammo warning still means only that.
Tapping the trigger no longer cuts the gun's wind-down off and starts it again. Each tap now winds down over the last one.
The game closes properly when SAPI 5 is what speaks. It could hang on the way out, and there was a click as it went.
With Modern audio output on, SAPI 5 starts talking sooner. It puts silence in front of every line it says - up to a tenth of a second of it, less the faster you have it set - and the game now drops that rather than playing it.
SAPI 5 answers as fast as a screen reader does. It used to make a piece of the line before you heard any of it, which cost longer the longer the line was; now it hands the game the voice as it makes it, so a word comes back in six milliseconds and a paragraph in twenty-three, where a paragraph used to take thirty-seven and a long sentence a tenth of a second.
Turning Modern audio output on while something was being read out no longer lets the old line carry on over the top of the new one for a fifth of a second.
Turning Modern audio output off takes hold the moment you press it. It waited for the next thing the game had to say, and the line announcing the change did not count, so it could still be playing SAPI 5 itself well after you turned it off.
If the sound device the speech is using goes away while you play - headphones unplugged, a controller with a speaker in it switched off, Windows moving to another one - the game opens another one and carries on. It used to lose the voice for the rest of the game, and with Modern audio output on that is every word it says.
In Settings, Speech, the Speech output row and the SAPI 5 voice row open a list instead of stepping. The cursor starts on the one you are using, Enter takes the one you land on, and Escape leaves it as it was. Stepping meant hearing every choice on the way past, which for the voices is however many your machine has.
Opening a weapon from the Loadout tab clicks, the way opening one in the Weapons tab or a power-up already did.

26.09.22-2:
You can turn with buttons on a game controller instead of a stick: the D-pad, left and right, to begin with. Settings, Joystick has them as Alternate turn left and Alternate turn right, and you can set them to any buttons you like. They turn at the same speed as the keyboard, and the sticks keep turning as they always did. When the tutorial names your controller, the aiming line names them too, by whatever you have set them to.
A new profile starts in Gesture mode instead of Button mode. If you have played before, the mode you are in is the one you had.
Melee weapons sound as they did before again. The swing and the hit both come from where you are, the way the original game has it, after the last version moved the hit out to the zombie you were hitting.
The menus are felt on a game controller: a click for each row or tab you land on, a firmer one when you press a button or change a setting, and two knocks for a screen - low to high going in, high to low coming back out. Joystick vibration scales them, and Off silences them.
The three vibration strengths feel further apart, and all of them are a little stronger. Medium and Strong used to be hard to tell apart.
Shooting a diamond and shooting a power-up container each have a feel of their own instead of a zombie's, and the power-up is felt again when it takes effect after the announcement. That one starts with the power-up's own sound, the deploy after the announcement, and lasts as long as it does - the Tesla coil going up, the Minigun spinning up - up to three seconds.
The Minigun is felt the whole time it fires, and a zombie it hits is felt over it instead of cutting it short. The Tesla coil has a crack of its own when it takes a zombie.
Some typing mistakes in the game's own writing are corrected.
The Miscellaneous tab has a Fine haptics option, on by default. With it on, a DualSense plugged in by USB plays what you feel in its grips; turn it off and it shakes its motors like any other controller. With no such controller connected the motors are used either way.
An explosion is felt on the controller from further away. It used to fade to almost nothing across the arena.
On the screen after a challenge you completed, the Retry button is read first, before Challenge selection and Next challenge, the way Try again comes first after a challenge you lost.
In Gesture mode, the tutorial line for the melee weapon says you can shake the controller as well, when the controller you have can be shaken.

26.09.22-1:
The game checks for updates by itself and tells you when one is out. An update downloads only the files that changed, so a small fix is a small download, and your progress, settings and keys are untouched.
The main menu has a Check for updates button, between Settings and Quit, for when you want to ask it yourself. It also tells you which version you are on. Run from source, it says updating is not available there instead, because a copy from GitHub updates with git.
When an update is offered, No now only means not now, and the game asks again the next time it starts. To stop being asked about that version, there is a new Skip this version button. A newer version is still offered, and Check for updates on the main menu still finds a skipped one.
The game keeps its version number inside itself now, not in a VERSION file beside it, so editing or deleting that file can no longer stop it from updating. A VERSION file left from an older version is not used any more.
The pause menu has a Restart challenge button, between Resume and End Game. A challenge you have already lost no longer has to be played to the end. Endless games do not have it.
The screen at the end of a run has a Copy results button, after an endless game and after a challenge you won or lost. It puts a short list of your results on the clipboard so you can paste them to somebody, and it comes before the other buttons.
Those screens read one result per line, the same as the copy. After Endless the first line is your tarot cards, and after a challenge it is the challenge name and whether you completed or failed it. The screen after a failed challenge reads out your kills, accuracy and time, which it never used to say.
The Play again button after an Endless game is called Close. It still takes you back to the Endless screen.
On the Endless screen, the Play button is dimmed along with the Armory and Back buttons while the cards are dealt, and all three are ready once the cards are down.
Changing a tarot card reads the new card out. Before, it changed in silence and you had to arrow off it and back to hear what you had.
On the tarot card screen, a card now tells you the right number of diamonds you have. After spending diamonds in the armory, it used to still say the old number.
In Info, the Statistics screen now counts the coins and diamonds you earn. Money earned and Diamonds collected always said 0 before.
After upgrading a power-up, the Upgrade button shows the new price at once. It used to offer the old one for about a second.
Melee is on both Control keys now by default, left and right, so whichever hand is free can reach it.
When a melee weapon connects, you hear the swing where you are and the hit out at the zombie you hit, instead of both in front of you. A swing that hits nothing is just the swing, from you.
Two zombies of the same kind no longer cut off each other's sound. One of them could go quiet and keep walking towards you.
The Shield zombie, and a few others, no longer die in silence when the killing hit is critical, which happened often with melee. Their hit sound was cut off and no death sound played.
When two zombies die close together at the end of a wave, the second one's death sound is no longer cut off.
After a revive, being killed by the same kind of zombie again plays its attack sound. It used to be silent, every time with the Shield zombie.
Page Up and Page Down set the volume of the menu music, on any menu. It goes from 100%, as loud as the music has always been, down to silent, and does nothing in a game.
The Menus tab in Settings is called Miscellaneous now and comes last. It has the menu layout, remember cursor position, tutorial text, which has moved there from the Sound tab, the joystick vibration and the trigger feel, the names in hints and tutorial, and the switch for checking for updates when the game starts.
The Miscellaneous tab has a Reset all settings button. It sets everything back to default, except your keys and your controller buttons.
You can play with a game controller, like a DualSense, DualShock, Xbox or Switch Pro controller. Either stick turns, slowly with a small push and fast when pushed all the way. R2 fires, R1 is melee, L1 switches weapon and L2 reloads, or in Gesture mode, flick a stick up or down. Options pauses.
A controller works in the menus too: the D-pad or a stick moves, Cross selects, Circle goes back, and L2 and R2 set the menu music volume.
Shaking the controller swings your melee weapon in Gesture mode, like shaking the phone did.
Settings has a Joystick tab. It names the controller you have connected and lets you change which button does what. Each kind of controller keeps its own buttons, and with more than one connected, the Controller row chooses which one you are setting. Connecting or unplugging a controller while the game is open updates the tab at once.
Joystick vibration and the trigger feel are in the Miscellaneous tab, with four settings each: Off, Light, Medium and Strong. Enter goes to the next and Shift+Enter to the previous. The trigger feel is only for a DualSense controller, and the option says so. Stepping it puts that feel on the triggers for a few seconds, so you can squeeze R2 and try it without starting a game.
A controller vibrates with the heartbeat when a zombie is close, harder the closer it is, and as hard as a hit hurts, so a big gun is felt more than a small one. It vibrates when a shot hits the Shield zombie's shield, when a zombie dies, for an explosion, harder the closer it is, when the tornado pushes zombies back, and when a zombie kills you.
On a DualSense, R2 is shaped like a pistol's trigger while you play: a long soft part with nothing in it, then a wall that holds, and pressing through the wall is what fires the shot. Letting the trigger back out to just under the wall is enough to fire again. In Button mode, L2 has a light pull for reloading.
A DualSense plugged in by USB plays the heartbeat you hear in its grips, using the fine haptics PlayStation games use instead of plain rumble.
The Miscellaneous tab has a Names in hints and tutorial option. Set it to Controller buttons and the hints and the tutorial text name the buttons of the controller you have connected instead of the keys.
Playing with the SAPI 5 voice instead of NVDA works like playing with NVDA. Before, the challenge screens said they were not ported yet, and the game itself was not described.
The game speaks through JAWS, ZDSR, Narrator, ZoomText, System Access and other screen readers too, not only NVDA. Before, it used the SAPI 5 voice with them. A screen reader started or closed while the game is open is followed within a few seconds.
Settings has a Speech tab. Speech output chooses who speaks: Automatic, the default, takes whichever screen reader is running, trying NVDA, JAWS, ZDSR, Narrator and then the others, or SAPI 5 when none is. Pick one instead and only that one speaks, so the game is silent while it is not running. Enter and Shift+Enter go through the list, and the option always tells you what you picked.
While SAPI 5 is what speaks, the Speech tab also has the SAPI 5 voice, rate, pitch and volume, and a rate boost for voices that can go faster. The voice starts on the one set in Control Panel, each change is said in SAPI 5 so you can hear it, and the rows come and go as a screen reader starts or closes.
